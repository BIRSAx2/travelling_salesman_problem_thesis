\section{Introduction}

This chapter presents a modified version of the \Gls{ACS} algorithm, called Red-Black Ant Colony System (\Gls{RB-ACS}), which aims to improve the performance of \Gls{ACS} on large-scale instances of the \Gls{TSP}. The \Gls{RB-ACS} incorporates several key improvements to the standard \Gls{ACS} approach, including parallel search, improved pheromone initialization, and differentiated parameter settings for the two ant groups. These modifications are designed to enable \Gls{RB-ACS} to explore the search space more effectively and efficiently converge towards optimal or near-optimal solutions, even for large-scale \Gls{TSP} problems \cite{Hassan2013}.

\section{The Red-Black Ant Colony System Algorithm (\Gls{RB-ACS})}
While the standard \Gls{ACS} algorithm has proven effective in solving \Gls{TSP} instances, its performance can deteriorate as problem size increases. The Red-Black Ant Colony System (\Gls{RB-ACS}) introduces several key modifications to the standard \Gls{ACS} approach to improve its scalability and solution quality for large-scale \Gls{TSP} problems \cite{Hassan2013}.

\subsection{Pheromone Initialization}
In standard \Gls{ACS}, the initial pheromone level on all edges is set to a constant value $\tau_0$. In \Gls{RB-ACS}, the authors propose a different pheromone initialization scheme, where the initial pheromone level on each edge $(r,s)$ is set according to the following equation:
\begin{equation}
	\tau_\text{init}(r,s) = \frac{C}{\text{cost}(r,s)}
\end{equation}
where $C$ is a constant and $\text{cost}(r,s)$ is the distance between cities $r$ and $s$. This modified pheromone initialization encourages ants to explore shorter edges, which are more desirable for the optimal tour, by assigning them higher initial pheromone levels \cite{Hassan2013}.

\subsection{Parallel Search with Red and Black Ant Groups}
Another key modification in the \Gls{RB-ACS} algorithm is the use of two separate ant groups, called "red" and "black", which explore the search space in parallel. In standard \Gls{ACS}, a single group of ants constructs tours and ants can follow the paths of other ants, which can lead to premature convergence and getting stuck in local minima.

In \Gls{RB-ACS}, the red and black ant groups operate independently, each maintaining their own pheromone trails and searching for solutions without being influenced by the other group. This parallel search approach reduces the likelihood that the algorithm gets stuck in local minima, as the two ant groups can explore different regions of the search space simultaneously \cite{Hassan2013}.

\subsection{Differentiated Parameter Settings}
In addition to the separate search process, the \Gls{RB-ACS} algorithm also employs different parameter settings for the red and black ant groups. Specifically, the local pheromone update rule and the pheromone evaporation rate can be set to different values for the two groups. This differentiation is inspired by the observed behavior of real ants, where different colonies or groups may exhibit distinct characteristics, such as pheromone deposition and evaporation rates \cite{Hassan2013}.

By using separate parameter settings for red and black ants, the \Gls{RB-ACS} algorithm can further enhance the diversification of the search process, allowing the two groups to explore the solution space in a more complementary manner.

\subsection{Improved Global Pheromone Update}
In standard \Gls{ACS}, only the globally best ant is allowed to deposit pheromones during the global update phase. In \Gls{RB-ACS}, the authors propose a modified global update rule, where the two best ants from each of the red and black groups are allowed to update the pheromone levels. This parallel global update further strengthens the search towards high-quality solutions, as multiple promising paths are reinforced simultaneously \cite{Hassan2013}.

\begin{algorithm}
	\caption{Red-Black Ant Colony System (\Gls{RB-ACS}) for the \Gls{TSP}}
	\begin{algorithmic}[1]
		\State Initialize pheromone levels $\tau_{ij} = \tau_{\text{init}}(i,j)$ for all edges $(i,j)$
		\State Initialize the best global solution $S_{gb}$ and its length $L_{gb}$
		\For{each iteration}
		\For{each ant group $g \in \{\text{red}, \text{black}\}$}
		\For{each ant $k = 1, \ldots, m_g$}
		\State Place ant $k$ from group $g$ on a random starting city
		\State Initialize partial tour $S_k^g = \emptyset$
		\Repeat
		\State Select the next city $j$ using the transition rule specific to group $g$
		\State Add $(i,j)$ to $S_k^g$
		\State Apply local pheromone update to $(i,j)$ according to group $g$ rules
		\State $i \gets j$
		\Until{tour $S_k^g$ is complete}
		\If{$L(S_k^g) < L_{gb}$}
		\State $S_{gb} \gets S_k^g$
		\State $L_{gb} \gets L(S_k^g)$
		\EndIf
		\EndFor
		\EndFor
		\For{each group $g \in \{\text{red}, \text{black}\}$}
		\State Select the two best ants from group $g$
		\State Apply global pheromone update to the tours of these ants
		\EndFor
		\EndFor
		\State \Return the best solution found $S_{gb}$
	\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Conclusion}

The \Gls{RB-ACS} algorithm presented in this chapter can be considered a promising approach for solving complex combinatorial optimization problems beyond the \Gls{TSP}, such as load balancing in telecommunication networks, economic load dispatch, process scheduling, and various other fields. The general principles of \Gls{RB-ACS}, including parallel search, differentiated parameter settings, and improved pheromone update strategies, can be potentially applied to a wide range of optimization problems, making it a valuable contribution in the field of swarm intelligence and metaheuristic algorithms.
