\chapter{Experimental Results}
\label{chapt:7}

In this chapter, an in-depth analysis of the experimental results obtained from the implementation and evaluation of different solution algorithms for the \textit{Travelling Salesman Problem} (TSP) is presented. The experiments were conducted on a wide range of problem instances, varying in size and complexity, to assess the performance of the algorithms in terms of solution quality, execution time, and scalability.

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\label{sec:metodologia}

\subsection{Setup}
The experiments were executed on a computing cluster (DEI Blade) with nodes equipped with Intel Xeon Gold 5118 CPU @ 2.30/3.20GHz processors and 128 GB of RAM. All algorithms were implemented in Rust.

\subsection{Problem Instances}
A diversified set of TSP instances was used, primarily drawn from TSPLIB \cite{TSPLIB}. The instances range from small-sized problems (51 cities) to large-scale problems (up to 5915 cities). Some of the instances used include:

\begin{itemize}
	\item eil51, berlin52, st70, eil76, pr76 (small-sized problems)
	\item lin105, pr124, lin318, rat575 (medium-sized problems)
	\item rat783, pr1002, pcb1173, d1655, fl1577, u2319 (large-sized problems)
	\item rl5915 (very large-scale problem)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implemented Algorithms}
The following algorithms were implemented and evaluated:

\begin{itemize}
	\item Nearest Neighbor (NN) and NN with 2-Opt (NN2Opt)
	\item Simulated Annealing (SA) and SA with 2-Opt (SA2Opt)
	\item Genetic Algorithm (GA) and GA with 2-Opt (GA2Opt)
	\item Ant Colony System (ACS) and ACS with 2-Opt (ACS2Opt)
	\item Red-Black Ant Colony System (RBACS) and RBACS with 2-Opt (RBACS2Opt)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
The main metrics used to evaluate algorithm performance are:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Execution time}: measured in milliseconds.
	\item \textbf{Solution quality}: length of the found tour.
	\item \textbf{Percentage gap}: calculated as $(length - optimum) / optimum * 100\%$, where "optimum" is the length of the known optimal tour for the instance.
\end{itemize}

	\item The basic algorithms (NN, SA, GA, ACS, RB-ACS) show significantly improved performance when combined with the 2-Opt local optimization procedure.

\begin{itemize}

	\item SA and SA2Opt maintain good solution quality even for large instances, but computation time grows rapidly.
	\item GA and GA2Opt show poor scalability both in terms of time and solution quality for very large instances.
	\item ACS, ACS2Opt, RBACS and RBACS2Opt offer the best compromise between execution time scalability and maintaining solution quality for large-sized instances.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussione}

The experimental results highlight several key points:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item The importance of local optimization: the addition of 2-Opt consistently improves the performance of all tested algorithms.
	\item The superiority of ant colony-based approaches (ACS and RBACS) for medium and large-sized instances.
	\item The variability of genetic algorithm performance, which suggests the need for careful parameter calibration to obtain good results.
	\item The trade-off between computation time and solution quality, with more sophisticated algorithms requiring more time but producing better solutions.
\end{enumerate}

However, the choice of the optimal algorithm depends on the specific requirements of the application, balancing the need for high-quality solutions with computation time constraints.

\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chapt:8}
In conclusion, this thesis has provided an in-depth and comparative analysis of different algorithmic approaches for solving the Travelling Salesman Problem, with particular attention to performance on large-sized instances. The results obtained not only confirm the effectiveness of well-established metaheuristic approaches such as Ant Colony System, but also introduce a variant, the Red-Black Ant Colony System, which shows potential for further improvements and applications.

\section{Final Reflections}
The evolution of algorithms for the TSP reflects a broader trend in the field of combinatorial optimization: the search for a balance between solution quality and computational efficiency. This thesis has demonstrated that:

\begin{itemize}
	\item There is no universally "best algorithm" for the TSP. The choice of the optimal approach depends on the specific characteristics of the problem, the instance size, and computational constraints.

	\item The hybridization of techniques, such as integrating local search procedures (2-Opt) into population-based metaheuristics, can lead to significant performance improvements.

	\item Scalability remains a crucial challenge, especially for very large-scale problems (over 10,000 cities). In this context, approaches like RBACS that offer greater parallelization opportunities become particularly relevant.

	\item Algorithm robustness, that is their ability to maintain good performance across a wide range of different instances, is a critical factor for practical applications.
\end{itemize}

\section{Concluding Remarks}
The Travelling Salesman Problem, despite its apparent simplicity, continues to challenge researchers and practitioners, pushing the boundaries of combinatorial optimization and theoretical computer science.
